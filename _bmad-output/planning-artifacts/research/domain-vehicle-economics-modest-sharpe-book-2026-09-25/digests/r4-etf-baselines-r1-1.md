### Synthesis (inference from the claims above, not a retrieved claim)
- Over the most recent decade, retrieved evidence puts realistic diversified trend at a Sharpe of about 0.0–0.4. The SG CTA Index had Sharpe 0.07 over Jul-2016 to Jun-2026 at about 3%/yr (claim 7). DBMF's best-case replicated strategy had 0.39 (claim 6). KMLM returned 0.42%/yr over 3 years (claim 2). The century-long backtests (claims 8 and 9) and the Faber in-sample Sharpe of 0.81 (claim 13) should be treated as upper bounds. Claims 10 and 11 argue that much of the classic TSMOM statistical evidence is fragile or comes from volatility scaling.
- On the "do-nothing" question: a 1099 ETF such as CTA, or KMLM and DBMF (whose tax forms were not confirmed on the pages fetched), gives futures-based long/short trend, including shorts and leverage, for 0.75–0.90%. A monthly long-only ETF TSMOM book at $25–50K cannot short, and it is closer to Faber's GTAA (claim 13) than to CTA-style trend. Wide dispersion across ETFs over the same period (KMLM 0.42% vs CTA 10.42% over 3y) means the choice of fund is itself a large, unhedged bet.
