NIST, Engineering Statistics Handbook, section 7.2.2.2: sample size requires assumptions about the alternative effect, variance, significance and missed-detection probability. Standardized effects permit conditional sizing without choosing a dollar variance. The known-variance one-sided formula is `(z(1-alpha)+z(target_power))^2/d^2`. Its unknown-variance discussion motivates accounting for finite-sample t critical values. SciPy, scipy.stats.nct reference: for independent standard-normal Y and chi-square V with k degrees of freedom, `(Y+c)/sqrt(V/k)` follows noncentral t with df=k, nc=c. Thus, under iid normal session outcomes, a one-sample t statistic under standardized effect d has df=n-1, nc=d*sqrt(n). The upper-tail probability at the central-t critical value yields conditional power. This derivation assumes normality and independence; it does not establish them for trading data.
